How isolation impacts brain development

Your brain is built to connect.

A teenage brain is still wiring itself, and other people are part of how it does that. Too much time alone changes the wiring. A little connection protects it. Here are six small ways to start.

Risk factor and protective factor

Risk factor

The risk: isolation

Spending a lot of time cut off from other people makes anxiety and depression more likely, and the teenage brain is especially sensitive to it (Loades et al., 2020; Orben et al., 2020).

Protective factor

The protection: connection

Supportive relationships with friends, family and trusted adults, and feeling that you belong at school, make those problems less likely (Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration, 2019).
